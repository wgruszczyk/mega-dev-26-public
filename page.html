5 reports read as a one-day move; filed under parser by tenant.

The row the table marks not this ticket — no ticket of its own is a second bug. The fix shipped on #4471 did not reach it.

The two

parser. Every tenant on file writes the day before the month. The shared parser reads every date month first, so the two swap. This happens on every channel, for every tenant, and it is what #4471 describes.

hint. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form then reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The date swaps again. This one needed the hint changed, not the parser, so it only reached reports that came in through form, from tenants on booking-v3.

What engineering did

#4471 closed on 5 October with fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. That is the parser cause, and it is fixed.

The hint cause closed as won’t fix: booking-v3 is retired. The cause leaves with the form, not with a fix, and the parser change never touched it. Anyone reading only #4471 would take “one issue, one fix” at face value and conclude the parser fix covered all of it. It covered the larger group. The smaller one was ended a different way, and nothing in #4471 says so.

Why they landed together

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone, so nobody reads the body. A wrong day looks the same from either bug, so both wrote the same subjects and both merged into the first ticket open. The rule did what it says; the subject line is just not the thing that separates these two.

The triage page that carries the rule is past the desk’s own staleness mark, and it is the page new people on the desk are told to start with.

Two things that move the counts

Intake tags are a guess. meta.intakeTag is read off the reporter’s browser the moment the report arrives. The cause depends on the tenant: the locale they write dates in and the form they run. The tagged as column above shows how often the two disagree, and it disagrees in both directions. A count taken from the tag lands in the wrong place for every one of them.

The export repeats itself. The ticket system exports in pages that overlap, so a report can appear twice under one id. The row count of the export file is higher than the number of reports. uniqueById in causes.ts is what makes the two agree.
